Science and Engineering

Kwon Ko
Jul 26, 2026

Any attempt to separate science and engineering, to rank them or treat one as subordinate to the other, is dangerous. Not because the distinction is meaningless, but because the moment you pick a side, you inherit that side's particular way of going wrong.

People say that science begins with a question, with curiosity, and that engineering begins with a problem. Taken as description, this is fine. Taken as identity, it becomes a way of excusing yourself from half the work. Rather than taking a side, I think what matters far more is holding both at once and making the directions they point converge into one.

For research that began as simple curiosity to carry real weight, it has to reach someone, eventually, as some form of usefulness. Not immediately, and not necessarily in the form its author imagined. But the obligation does not expire just because the timeline is long. Curiosity earns its keep by arriving somewhere.

When we set out to solve a problem, we should ask the same kind of question in reverse. What changes if this is solved? What does it do to people? Did this need solving at all? What was my own motive in starting it? Did it have to be this problem, of all problems? That interior intent is what matters, and it is the part nobody reviews for you.

Science that never reaches usefulness stays unfinished on its own terms. Engineering that offers no meaning is easily corrupted. These are not the same failure, and neither one is a milder version of the other. They are what each side looks like when it stops listening to the other.

When I meet someone who leans hard to one side and insists that only their side is right, I think of John 9:25. Whether he is a sinner I do not know. One thing I do know is: whereas once I was blind, but now I can see. I am not a religious person. But what the verse says is clear enough. Whether that man is right or wrong, I do not know. What I know is only the one thing I lived through myself. The certainty that one side is correct is a certainty no person is in a position to hold.

Humans are small and faint. We take hold of what lies within reach of our knowing, and we think about it, and think about it again, and dig deeper. The idea that doing so will someday bring us to the truth is probably an illusion. And yet not stopping that illusion, and believing that science and engineering are not two different things. Continuing to keep faith with it while knowing it cannot be reached. That, I think, is the only truth I have.
